Getting Started

These exercises cover two starting scenarios. In the first, the attacker already has access to the system through valid credentials or an existing foothold. In the second, the attacker starts without system access and tries to gain entry or disrupt services. We organize the modules by the attacker’s access at the start of the exercise.

Module 0 — Connect to your assigned VM provides your group’s credentials and connection instructions so you can open your assigned VM desktop. Module 1 — Attacker already has system access focuses on investigating changes, containing unauthorized activity and recovering services. Module 2 — Attacker starts without system access focuses on detecting attempts, reducing exposure and maintaining availability. Use the exercise workflow below, then choose your group’s Linux or Windows tasks.

Exercise workflow

  1. Assign teams: divide each group into a red team and a blue team for the selected exercise.
  2. Attack: the red team performs the assigned attack on its group’s VM.
  3. Investigate and recover: the blue team identifies what happened, examines and preserves evidence, contains the attack, and restores the affected services. Verify that normal service and legitimate access work again.
  4. Reverse roles: after recovery, the red team becomes the blue team and the blue team becomes the red team. Repeat the exercise so everyone practices both attack and defense.
  5. Optional platform swap: after both rounds, the Linux and Windows groups may exchange platforms. Use the new platform’s assigned credentials and tasks, and repeat the red-team/blue-team rounds.

Finish recovery and restore the exercise starting conditions before reversing roles or swapping platforms.

Connect from macOS

  1. Open Microsoft Windows App on your Mac (available from the Mac App Store), then select + → Add PC.
  2. Enter your assigned VM’s connection address from the Linux group table or Windows group table in PC name. Set Gateway to No gateway, or leave it unset.
  3. Save the connection and open it to sign in. Enter your assigned group username and password. Follow the remote-login note in your group’s table.

Connect from Windows

  1. On your Windows computer, press Windows+R, type mstsc, and press Enter.
  2. Enter your assigned VM’s connection address from the Linux group table or Windows group table in Computer. Set Gateway to No gateway, or leave it unset.
  3. Select Connect. Choose More choices → Use a different account if needed. Enter your assigned group username and password. Follow the remote-login note in your group’s table.

Connect from Linux

  1. On your Linux computer, open Remmina and create a connection using the RDP protocol.
  2. Enter your assigned VM’s connection address from the Linux group table or Windows group table in Server. Set Gateway to No gateway, or leave it unset.
  3. Save the connection and connect. Enter your assigned group username and password. Follow the remote-login note in your group’s table.

If a certificate warning appears, ask the instructor to confirm the certificate. If you cannot connect, give the instructor the error message and your assigned VM.
